# World lighting

`Resources/WorldLighting.asset` sets the fixed street-light and power-light pools. Each mode points to a `LightingCycle` in `Resources/Lighting`; each cycle references `LightingPreset` assets. Hero and Hero Endless hold Day, Villain holds Dusk, Villain Endless holds Night, and Free Play advances through all three. `StaticDay` has preview keys for Dusk/Night but its clock does not advance during play. `WorldLightingDirector` applies the cycle to the one scene directional light, ambient probe, fog, a session copy of the procedural sky, and emission on existing shared `CityPalette` materials. It selects nearby intact street lamps from the generated city; it does not change `CityLayout` or create city materials. Street and power lights are preallocated, capped at four each by default, and have no shadows.

Tune the looks in `Lighting/Day.asset`, `Dusk.asset`, and `Night.asset`, the shared material swatches in `CityPalette.asset`, and light caps/ranges in `WorldLighting.asset`. New modes should reference a cycle asset. New power definitions can enable their `Light` spec: instant powers flash on a successful, equipped use; a traveling projectile can also call `FollowPower` from its effect. Fire does this now. All requests pass through the same fixed pool. `LightingSetup.Configure` authors missing assets and links the shipping modes without replacing existing tuned assets; a second run was byte-identical.

Run `LightingVerification.Run` in Unity batch mode for the same-view 1280×720 day/dusk/night captures and a real Fire cast, a four-light stress case, and every mode's cycle. `CityArtVerification.Run` protects shared materials, generated art, and the exact legacy daytime sun/ambient values. Results and render captures are in `Verification/Lighting`. Batch Editor FPS is a comparison aid, not a standalone-player target; judge night readability and moment-to-moment lighting in a hands-on pass.
